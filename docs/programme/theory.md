# Programme Theory

## Function of theory

Theory has two roles in this programme:

- mechanism-facing theory identifies a relation worth representing and an observable consequence;
- interpretive theory supplies disciplined, competing readings of patterns after the mechanism has
  been verified.

Neither role supplies numerical calibration without direct calibration evidence. A citation cannot
authorize a mechanism by itself, and an interpretive category cannot be substituted for a missing
social relation.

## Generative and complexity modelling

Agent-based modelling asks whether explicit local rules are sufficient to generate a macro-pattern.
It does not establish that the same pattern in the world has the same cause. The model therefore
retains complete transition evidence, separates seed variation from parameter variation, and reports
the domain where a pattern reproduces.

## Ecology and disturbance

Project 1 represents a renewable spatial stock, heterogeneous baseline capacity, extraction,
stochastic disturbance, and finite recovery. Pulse/press distinctions and spatial-disturbance research
motivate separating event timing, footprint, damage, and recovery. Linear recovery, damage fractions,
and wavefront rules remain project choices. Ecological “recovery debt” motivates cumulative deficit
measurement, not empirical equivalence between resource stock and biodiversity or ecosystem
function.

## Distribution and inequality

Distribution is a primary outcome rather than a side statistic. Project 1 measures unequal harvest,
energy, unmet need, downside exposure, and persistence while keeping these objects distinct. Material
rank persistence is evidence of a durable model outcome, not wealth ownership, social class, or a
historical regime.

## Classical-Marxian and radical political economy

These traditions foreground production relations, ownership, exploitation, accumulation, conflict,
and control. Project 1 contains none of their defining social relations. It can show unequal material
outcomes under ecological competition; it cannot show class exploitation or capitalist accumulation.
Projects 2 and 3 may examine informational advantage, conditional access, and dependency, but those
remain narrower categories until control over others' feasible action is represented.

## Ecological Marxism

Ecological materialism directs attention to material throughput, ecological degradation, and the
social organization of human–nature relations. Project 1 can measure extraction and recovery deficit.
It cannot demonstrate a metabolic rift because it contains no capitalist production relation,
spatially organized exchange, or historically specific separation of producers from conditions of
production.

## Substantive economic anthropology

A substantive approach treats provisioning as socially instituted rather than assuming that all
allocation is market exchange. This is mainly a boundary discipline in Project 1, which contains
individual extraction but no reciprocity, redistribution, prices, or obligations. Project 3's D0–D3
probes compare social forms of information circulation without calling credits money or accounted
transfer a market.

## Human behavioural ecology

Human behavioural ecology and foraging theory motivate questions about resource distributions,
mobility, risk, energetic shortfall, and adaptive strategies. Their optimality assumptions and
fitness interpretations do not automatically apply to the literal fixed policy or abstract energy
stock. Project 1 therefore uses “subsistence security” and “material outcome,” not biological fitness.

## Information and adaptive decision-making

Project 2 isolates an observation channel `C = (radius, noise, delay)` before adding private tabular
learning. Information has value only through its effect on a decision under a specified policy and
environment. Better sensing can fail to improve outcomes, and average gains can coexist with more
unequal conversion of opportunity.

## Cultural evolution and social learning

Project 3 distinguishes simple exposure from reinforced multi-source diffusion and retains a
no-diffusion control. Social transmission can spread useful or misleading information and can
homogenize or stratify capability. Diffusion is not assumed to be cumulative culture, institutional
formation, or collective intelligence.

## Embeddedness and network economics

Project 3 represents realized ties, directional trust, reports with source timestamps, and optional
rewiring. Network position can alter information access. Centrality or source concentration indicates
relational structure; it is not automatically power. “Gatekeeping potential” is permitted only when
counterfactual report removal shows asymmetric dependency.

## Gift, reciprocity, and risk pooling

Costly contribution, immediate accounted transfer, and deferred obligation are distinct mechanisms.
An unconditional or costly report is not automatically a gift in the full anthropological sense;
repeated balanced transfer is not sufficient to infer reciprocity; and Project 3 does not pool
material loss. Risk-pooling theory is used as boundary and comparative interpretation.

## Credit, debt, and obligation

Project 3 information credits are conserved access tokens. An obligation is a claim recorded in a
separate ledger. Creating or defaulting on a claim never creates or destroys credits. This minimal
temporal accounting can produce access dependency but does not reproduce money, interest-bearing
capital, debt markets, collateral, inheritance, or anthropological debt generally.

## Evidence discipline

The [theory matrix](theory_matrix.md) states the correlate, observable, required and absent
mechanisms, licensed interpretation, prohibited inference, and evidence role for every active concept.
The [interpretation protocol](interpretation_protocol.md) applies these distinctions to results.
