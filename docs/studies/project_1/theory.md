# Project 1 Theory

## Generative question

Project 1 tests whether ecological differences and material competition are sufficient to generate
unequal and persistent outcomes before information inequality or social organization is introduced.
“Sufficient in the model” is narrower than a historical causal claim.

## Ecology and disturbance

Pulse/press ecology motivates separating immediate damage from recovery dynamics. Landscape
disturbance research motivates independent, spatially propagated, and system-correlated treatments.
Recovery-debt research motivates integrating deficits over time. None of these sources calibrates the
project's multiplicative damage, Bernoulli hazards, wavefront, or linear recovery.

## Spatial opportunity and mobility

Social-ecological ABM research shows that resource heterogeneity and mobility can interact
nonlinearly. Project 1 therefore estimates their joint effects rather than assuming that mobility is
always adaptive. The literal policy is deliberately bounded: it sees only local stock and moves
randomly when the threshold is not met.

## Distribution and persistence

Distributional economics motivates reporting agent-level incidence alongside aggregates. The
enduring-inequality literature motivates distinguishing temporary dispersion from persistent rank,
but its historical mechanisms—property, inheritance, elite power, and coercive labour—are absent.
Project 1 measures material rank persistence, not wealth-class reproduction.

## Subsistence security

Anthropological work on subsistence and livelihood risk motivates distinguishing mean provisioning
from downside exposure. Shortfall frequency, spells, depth, catastrophic events, and survival remain
separate. The model contains no moral norm, entitlement, household, sharing institution, or biological
fitness measure.

## Ecological-materialist boundary

Extraction and recovery deficit are material-flow outcomes. They may be discussed through ecological
materialism, but the model has no capitalist production relation or historically specific metabolism.
Resource depletion is therefore not evidence of a metabolic rift.

## Interpretive standard

Every result receives mechanical, computational, economic, political-economic, anthropological, and
boundary readings under the programme protocol. Competing explanations and prohibited conclusions
are part of the result, not optional discussion.
