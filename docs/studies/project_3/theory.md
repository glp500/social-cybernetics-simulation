# Project 3 Theory

## Embedded social access

Project 3 treats information access as relational: a receiver's actionable opportunity depends on
which sources it can reach, the age/content of their reports, and its directional trust. Embeddedness
illuminates this structure but does not imply that the minimal network reproduces a real economy.

## Network learning tradeoffs

Network research motivates comparing private sensing with fixed social reporting and fixed with
adaptive topology. Connectivity can improve access while increasing source concentration,
homogenization, or correlated error. No topology is presumed universally efficient.

## Cultural evolution and diffusion

Simple and reinforced exposure distinguish one-source transmission from complex contagion. Adoption of
a complete sensing profile is a minimal capability-diffusion mechanism. It is not cumulative culture,
teaching, imitation of a social identity, or institutionalization.

## Trust and dependency

Directional trust is a model weight updated from validated report accuracy. Endogenous source choice
can create concentration and counterfactual dependency. Centrality, concentration, and dependency are
not automatically power because senders have only the bounded discretion specified by circulation
rules.

## Social forms of circulation

D0–D3 distinguish unconditional transfer, costly contribution, immediate accounted access, and a
minimal deferred claim. Economic anthropology motivates treating these as different social forms
rather than reducing them to price. The experiment does not presume that D1 is a gift, D2 a market,
or D3 anthropological debt.

## Risk and resilience

Social reports may spread ecological risk information and alter recovery after shocks. Risk-pooling
theory supplies boundary and comparative questions, but the model pools no material loss and creates
no need-based transfer institution.

## Political-economic boundary

Concentrated sources, accumulated credits, or obligations may produce informational advantage,
dependency, or gatekeeping potential. Class, exploitation, commodification, and power require stronger
relations of ownership, appropriation, and control than this study contains.
