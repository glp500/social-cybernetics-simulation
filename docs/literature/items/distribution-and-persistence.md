# Distribution and Persistence of Material Outcomes

## Sources

- Piketty, T. 2015. “Putting Distribution Back at the Center of Economics.” *Journal of Economic
  Perspectives* 29: 67–88. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1257/jep.29.1.67)
- Bowles, S., and M. Fochesato. 2024. “The Origins of Enduring Economic Inequality.” *Journal of
  Economic Literature* 62: 1475–1537. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1257/jel.20241718)

## Interpretive support

Piketty supports treating distributions and their distinct causes as primary analytical objects rather
than reporting only aggregates. Bowles and Fochesato distinguish the appearance of unequal outcomes
from the historical conditions that allow substantial wealth inequality to endure. Together they
motivate measuring named distributions, rank transitions, duration, and decay.

## Counterevidence and boundary conditions

Both sources address historical economic quantities and institutions absent from Project 1. Bowles
and Fochesato emphasize property-relevant technologies, egalitarian culture, elite power, and coerced
labour as conditions for enduring historical wealth inequality. Project 1 has no property, inheritance,
production class, political authority, labour relation, or intergenerational transmission.

## Licensed use

- measure harvest, energy, and unmet-need distributions separately;
- distinguish transient dispersion from persistent cumulative-harvest rank;
- analyze aggregates and incidence together;
- state which absent mechanisms prevent a historical inequality interpretation.

## Does not license

- interpreting harvest or energy as income, wealth, or capital;
- translating persistent material rank into class;
- claiming that ecological competition explains enduring historical inequality;
- calibrating inequality thresholds, quartiles, or experimental horizons.

## Project choices

Top/bottom shares, rank checkpoints, quartile transitions, advantage spells, half-life convention,
seed count, and 1,000-tick horizon are transparent analytical design choices.
