# Spatial Heterogeneity and Mobility

## Sources

- Pérez-Ibarra, I., and M. A. Janssen. 2015. “The Effect of Spatial Heterogeneity and Mobility on the
  Performance of Social–Ecological Systems.” *Ecological Modelling* 296: 1–11.
  [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ecolmodel.2014.10.014)
- Little, L. R., and A. D. McDonald. 2007. “Simulations of Agents in Social Networks Harvesting a
  Resource.” *Ecological Modelling* 204: 379–386.
  [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ecolmodel.2007.01.013)

## Mechanism support

Pérez-Ibarra and Janssen directly model mobile agents harvesting a spatially heterogeneous renewable
resource and report nonlinear effects of mobility and landscape richness. This supports making
heterogeneity × mobility an explicit experiment rather than treating either as a nuisance setting.
Little and McDonald support renewable-resource harvesting as a useful ABM substrate and later motivate
the independent Project 3 social-information comparison.

## Counterevidence and boundary conditions

The Pérez-Ibarra/Janssen agents include cooperative and selfish harvesting strategies, settlements,
and a sustainability framing absent from Project 1. Their result directions therefore cannot be
transferred to the literal harvest-or-random-move policy. Little and McDonald's uncertainty and
network mechanism belongs to Project 3, not the ecological control.

## Licensed use

- test the interaction of mean-preserving heterogeneity, population pressure, and movement cost;
- report ecological, aggregate, distributional, and security outcomes jointly;
- expect possible nonlinear or adverse mobility effects rather than presuming optimization.

## Does not license

- calling the fixed policy cooperative, selfish, optimal, or empirically realistic;
- using the published parameter values as calibration;
- importing social networks or governance into Project 1;
- predicting the sign of the Project 1 contrasts.

## Project choices

The 5×5 landscape, `5/15` checkerboard, population counts, movement costs, positions, seeds, and
horizons are controlled design choices.
