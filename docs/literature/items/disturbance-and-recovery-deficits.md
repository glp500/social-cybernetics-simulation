## Mechanism and measurement support

Bender and colleagues distinguish pulse from sustained perturbation and motivate studying relaxation
after damage. Turner and colleagues support treating disturbance spread as a spatial process. Massie
and colleagues support distinguishing spatially independent from correlated environmental forcing.
Moreno-Mateos and colleagues show why endpoint recovery can conceal accumulated interim deficits.

## Counterevidence and boundary conditions

These studies concern species/community dynamics, landscape disturbance, environmental synchrony, or
empirical ecosystem functions. Project 1 has one abstract resource field. Its Bernoulli event clock,
Von Neumann synchronous wavefront, multiplicative damage, simultaneous compounding, and exact linear
recovery are not derived from these sources.

## Licensed use

- compare independent, connected, and system-correlated shock structures;
- separate damage to current stock, capacity, and regeneration;
- retain complete recovery histories and cumulative normalized deficits;
- distinguish initial incidence from propagated exposure.

## Does not license

- calling model stock biodiversity, biomass, carbon, nitrogen, or ecosystem function;
- treating linear recovery or any parameter as calibrated;
- assuming recovery to baseline erases accumulated ecological burden;
- predicting empirical disturbance frequency or geometry.

## Project choices

Every shock probability, damage fraction, spread rule, recovery duration, matching convention, and
combined deficit calculation is a versioned project choice.
