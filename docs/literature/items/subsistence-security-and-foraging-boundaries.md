  [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1002/(SICI)1520-6505(2000)9:2%3C51::AID-EVAN1%3E3.0.CO;2-7)

## Interpretive support

Scott places subsistence security and downside risk at the centre of agrarian political response.
Human behavioural ecology provides a comparative framework for resource choice, mobility, risk, and
adaptive strategy. These sources motivate measuring frequency, duration, and depth of energetic
shortfall rather than treating mean harvest as welfare.

## Counterevidence and boundary conditions

The optimal-foraging review explicitly warns about simple energy currencies, risk, realism, and scale.
Scott's moral economy includes peasant households, norms, obligations, elites, and political response;
none exists in Project 1. Model energy is neither biological fitness nor an empirically mapped
subsistence requirement.

## Licensed use

- use “subsistence security” as a model-bound outcome family with an explicit viability target;
- analyze mobility and extraction under ecological constraints;
- report downside risk beside efficiency;
- consider HBE and livelihood interpretations alongside their missing assumptions.

## Does not license

- claiming optimal foraging, reproductive fitness, peasant behaviour, or moral economy;
- treating the viability target as an empirical poverty/subsistence line;
- using ethnographic theory to calibrate basal cost or energy conversion;
- inferring household or collective risk-sharing relations.

## Project choices

The viability target, catastrophic threshold, spell censoring, costs, and harvest conversion are
controlled abstractions.
